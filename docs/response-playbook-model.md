# ORVIKA Response playbook model

Status: offline educational guidance; source-level implementation

ORVIKA provides five curated playbooks: account compromise, payment fraud, lost device, suspected malware and identity exposure. Each playbook separates an immediate first action, ordered steps, useful information to preserve, actions to avoid, escalation criteria and important limitations.

## Escalation guidance

Escalation criteria help users recognise when self-help is insufficient and which type of provider, organisation, authority or qualified professional may need to be contacted. They are intentionally jurisdiction-neutral. Users must obtain contact details from independently verified official sources.

- Account compromise: service provider, organisation security team and financial provider where relevant.
- Payment fraud: bank/payment provider immediately, plus the appropriate official reporting channel.
- Lost device: local authority, mobile carrier and employer/school where managed data is involved.
- Suspected malware: qualified incident-response support, organisational security team and affected providers.
- Identity exposure: issuing authority, financial institution, privacy regulator or law-enforcement channel as applicable.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

- Preserve original messages, transaction references, timestamps, alerts and relevant records where safe and lawful.
- Avoid deleting or altering potentially relevant evidence before recording it.
- Do not recommend confronting suspects, paying recovery agents, using unverified contact details or installing unsolicited tools.
- A suspected incident is not proof of compromise or malware.
- ORVIKA does not detect incidents, inspect devices, freeze accounts, contact providers or authorities, submit reports, recover funds or provide legal/financial advice.
- Reporting procedures, deadlines and remedies vary by jurisdiction. The app does not hard-code emergency or cybercrime contact numbers.

## Maintenance

Review playbook accuracy when platform recovery flows, payment processes or reporting guidance materially change. Translations require subject-matter and native-speaker review before being described as approved. This content is general education, not a substitute for qualified incident response.
